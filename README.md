# Wisp

**A Mac companion for people who build.** Wisp started as a notch app. It now runs your coding agents, shows live activities and keeps your files, clipboard, music and day within reach, from the notch, a floating island or orb, or an ordinary window. Its AI runs on your Mac, and it has no account and no analytics.

## At a glance

- **Coding agents:** Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Copilot CLI, Cursor and OpenCode sessions in one place, with Allow and Deny cards, answers to Claude's questions, always-allow rules, usage limits and spend, session history and a jump to the exact terminal pane. Approvals can also come to your phone or Apple Watch, and cloud agent tasks and their pull request checks have a lane of their own.
- **Wherever you want it:** the notch, a drawn notch or island on other displays, a corner, an edge, the bottom of the screen, a floating orb you tear off the notch, or a resizable window with a sidebar and a full page for every widget. A radial ring puts eight actions at the pointer.
- **Live activities:** music with lyrics, timers and Pomodoro, calls with mute, camera and leave controls, downloads, device batteries, AirPods, volume and brightness, a watched window region and pills from your own scripts.
- **Shelf and tools:** 17 widgets, a file tray with AirDrop, convert and zip, clipboard history, per-app volume, Up Next, notifications with quick replies, and profiles that switch with the app you're in.
- **On-device AI and input:** Apple Intelligence or Ollama on your Mac for text actions and PDF summaries, quick add for timers, reminders and events, push-to-talk dictation, and text or QR capture from the screen.
- **Automation:** the `wisp` command, `wisp://` links and Shortcuts actions.

The full list is under [Features](#features).

## Install

Download `Wisp-<version>.dmg` from [Releases](https://github.com/Maestroharyor/wisp/releases), open it and drag Wisp to Applications.

Builds are not notarised yet, so the first launch is blocked:
1. Open Wisp once. macOS says it can't verify the developer.
2. Go to System Settings › Privacy & Security and click **Open Anyway** next to Wisp.

Or run `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Wisp.app` once. A first-launch setup then walks you through Accessibility, how the shelf opens and connecting your agents.

With Homebrew, each release also carries a cask file (`wisp.rb`) for a personal tap.

## Updates

Wisp updates itself with [Sparkle](https://sparkle-project.org) from GitHub Releases.

- **Daily check.** On its second launch Wisp asks whether to check for updates automatically; you can change this in Settings › About. When on, it reads `appcast.xml` from the latest release once a day. When there's a newer version, a window shows the release notes with Install, Skip and Remind Me Later.
- **By hand.** Choose **Check for Updates…** from the menu bar icon, or **Check Now** in Settings › About.
- **Settings › About › Updates** has two switches: check automatically, and download and install automatically. With the second on, Wisp downloads in the background and installs the next time it quits.
- **Installing.** Sparkle checks the download's EdDSA signature against the key built into Wisp, swaps the app in place and relaunches it. Updates don't need "Open Anyway".
- **Permissions are kept.** Every release is signed with the same certificate, so Accessibility and the other grants carry over. The one exception is moving to the `com.ayomideodewale.wisp` bundle ID (0.2.0): macOS ties permissions to the bundle ID, so grant them once more after that update. Your settings are copied over from the old ID automatically.

## Features

- **Coding agents in the notch**
  - Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Copilot CLI, Cursor and OpenCode, connected from setup or Settings › Agents. Wisp edits `~/.claude/settings.json`, `~/.codex/hooks.json`, `~/.gemini/settings.json` or `~/.cursor/hooks.json` and keeps a backup; for Copilot CLI it writes a hooks file of its own, `~/.copilot/hooks/wisp.json`, and for OpenCode a plugin of its own, `~/.config/opencode/plugins/wisp.js`, which it deletes to disconnect.
  - Gemini CLI is monitor only: its sessions, Done and tool activity show in the notch, and when it asks you to confirm a tool call the session shows Needs you until you answer in the terminal and the tool runs. Its hooks can't approve a call on your behalf, so there's no Allow or Deny card, and no cost, context or usage limits.
  - Cursor (the editor's agent and the `cursor-agent` command line) is monitor only too: its sessions, prompts, Done and compaction show in the notch, and with tool activity on each tool shows in the pill once it finishes. Cursor's hooks can block a command but can't approve one, and it sends no hook while it waits for your approval, so there's no Allow or Deny card and no Needs you; answer in Cursor. No cost, context or usage limits either. Cursor also runs Claude Code's hooks by default; Wisp ignores those calls, so a Cursor session never shows up as Claude Code.
  - Copilot CLI is monitor only too: its sessions, Done and tool activity show in the notch, and when it shows a permission prompt the session shows Needs you until you answer in the terminal. Copilot's approval hook runs before its own rules and "allow for this session" choices, so answering from the notch would ask you about every command and edit Copilot already lets through; Wisp leaves approvals to the terminal. No cost, context or usage limits.
  - OpenCode's permission prompts get an Allow or Deny card too, while OpenCode asks in the terminal as well: answer in either place and the other goes away. "Deny with a reason…" sends your words to the model; a plain Deny is the same as rejecting in OpenCode. Its questions show Needs you until you answer them in the terminal. Restart OpenCode after connecting so it loads the plugin. No cost, context or usage limits yet, and requests from subagents show under the session that started them.
  - A pill while a session works, Done when it finishes, and Needs you when it waits. With several sessions going, the most urgent one (just finished or waiting on you, then working) takes the pill and a "+N" badge counts the rest. Clicking the pill opens the Agents view on that session, whatever your Home holds.
  - **Tool activity** (opt-in, Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Copilot CLI, Cursor and OpenCode): the pill shows what the session is doing, such as a terminal icon with `npm test` or a pencil with the file being edited, and the details list the current tool, tools used and subagents running. Secrets in commands (tokens, passwords, API key headers, `-u user:password`, keys in URLs) are masked. Settings › Agents › Advanced › "Show what agents are doing" adds hooks that hand each call to Wisp and return at once, so a tool call never waits for Wisp.
  - Permission prompts become an Allow, Allow All or Deny card (a bar when the shelf is open), with a sound you can change in Settings › Agents › Sounds. Several at once queue up ("1 of 2") instead of replacing each other; prompts you leave fall back to the terminal, and the session shows Needs you. Answer a prompt in the terminal instead and its card goes on its own; Terminal, beside Deny, hands it back there and brings that terminal forward, and the chevron minimises it into the pill, which shows a violet count and a pulsing ring until you click it or press an approval key. A Claude Code plan gets an Approve or Deny card of its own. Preview opens the card to show the edit as a coloured unified diff of up to 40 lines (each MultiEdit edit under its own header, a Codex patch as it's written), the start of a new file, the whole command or the plan, and "Deny with a reason…" tells Claude what to do instead (hidden in private mode).
  - **Always allow…** (Claude Code, from the menu beside Allow All) saves a rule such as `Bash(npm test *)` or `Edit(src/**)` in Claude Code's own `permissions.allow`, so it holds even when Wisp isn't running. Wisp suggests a rule no wider than the request, and you can edit it, then choose This project (`.claude/settings.local.json` at the repository root) or Everywhere (`~/.claude/settings.json`, where a file rule names the project's folder so it doesn't apply to every project). Prefix rules stop short of commands that push, reset, publish or delete, which are matched exactly. Your user settings are backed up first; a project's local file gets no copies beside it, so nothing extra lands in the repository. Settings › Agents › Rules lists the allow, ask and deny rules for Everywhere and for each project with a live session, and edits or removes allow rules.
  - **Rule choices:** for a single shell command the menu lists rules from narrowest to widest, such as `Bash(git log --oneline -5)`, `Bash(git log --oneline *)` and `Bash(git log *)`, each with "Matches N of your last 50 requests" counted from the approval history. Picking one opens the same Always allow window with it filled in. Wisp never offers the program alone (`Bash(git *)`), commands that push or delete stay exact, and compound commands or ones with `$(…)` get no choices (Always allow… still covers them). The choices spell out the command, so private mode shows only Always allow….
  - **Approval history:** every request, plan and question is kept with how it ended: Allowed, Denied, Answered, Answered in the terminal, Allow All or Timed out. The history button's Approvals tab filters them by session and outcome, and each session's details list its own. The last 7 days are kept, at most 200, with the card's masked one-line summary and nothing more; the summary is left out while private mode hides details.
  - **Questions:** when Claude asks you something (AskUserQuestion), the card shows its options. Click one to answer, pick several and Send for multiple choice, type under Other…, or press Terminal to answer there (it brings that terminal forward). Long options wrap onto up to three rows, and scroll past that. Several questions in one ask step through ("1 of 2") and go back together.
  - **Keyboard answers:** while a card shows, Caps Lock + Y allows (or approves a plan), Caps Lock + N denies, and Caps Lock + 1–9 picks a question's option (Caps Lock + Y then sends a multiple choice answer). Without Caps Lock as Hyper, use ⌃⌥⌘ with the same keys; Wisp holds only the keys the card on screen uses (Y and N for a request or plan, Y and 1–9 for a question), and only while it shows. Each press answers once: holding a key never answers the next card, and you let go before the same key answers again. Keys pressed in the first moment after a card or the next question appears are ignored, like clicks, and so are keys while the card is covered (for example by the volume display).
  - Each session shows the icon of the terminal or editor it runs in (Ghostty, Warp, VS Code...), and the shelf's Terminal button is named after it.
  - **Jump to the exact pane:** clicking a session brings its app forward at once, then selects its tmux pane (switching window, and the tmux client's session if needed) and its tab in iTerm2, Terminal, Ghostty (1.3 or later) or WezTerm, and brings the app forward again. Kitty, Warp and editors only come forward as an app. Sessions in an editor skip tmux, and so does a pane that isn't the agent's own terminal (a shell started from tmux carries its variables along). Wisp reads your login shell's PATH once, when the first session shows up, to find `tmux` and `wezterm`. The first jump into iTerm2, Terminal or Ghostty asks for Automation access to that app, and Wisp waits up to a minute for your answer; declining still brings the app forward.
  - A session leaves the notch when its agent quits, even without saying so (a closed terminal tab, a crash): Wisp checks every 15 seconds whether the agent's process is still running.
  - Each project gets its own colour; each session shows its tokens and cost. Claude Code reports its own cost, model and lines changed; otherwise the cost is estimated from the transcript.
  - A ring around the agent's mark shows how full the context window is (amber from 70% and red from 90% by default; Settings › Agents › Warn at and Critical at), with an alert when it passes Warn at and a "Compacting…" pill while the context is compacted. Codex's context is followed live while a turn runs: Wisp watches its session file and reads the newest figures at most once a second, instead of only when the turn ends.
  - **Compact now:** when a Claude Code or Codex session passes Critical at, a card offers Compact now (and the session's details do while its ring is red). It brings the session's terminal forward and pastes `/compact` without pressing Return, so you send it; your clipboard comes back afterwards. Codex runs `/compact` only between turns. Needs Accessibility, as pasting does.
  - **Spend cap** (off by default): Settings › Agents › Alerts › "Pause a session at a spend cap" sets an amount per session. When a session reaches it, a card asks Keep going, Raise (by the same amount again) or Stop, and that session's next requests wait off screen until you choose. Stop denies them and presses Esc in its terminal to interrupt it (Accessibility). Codex's spend is an estimate from its tokens, and the card says so; Claude Code without a status line Wisp reads is checked when a turn ends. Allow All doesn't get past the cap, and closing the card or turning the cap off counts as Keep going.
  - **Sounds:** Settings › Agents › Sounds has a switch, a sound and a play button for Needs approval, Question, Finished, Failed (the agent quit without ending its session), Context warning, Context critical and Spend cap. Pick any macOS sound or Choose file… for your own; a burst of requests from one session chimes once. "Mute during calls or Focus" keeps them quiet while a call app has the microphone or a Focus is on (Focus is read only with Full Disk Access, as notifications need), and the volume is under Advanced. The old permission sound switch and any sound pack you used for agents carry over.
  - **Idle nudge** (on, after 5 minutes): a session still waiting on you gets one more nudge per wait: its sound again, one bigger pulse of a minimised card's pill with "Claude Code has waited 5 min" in it (or that line beside the notch for a prompt in the terminal), and the same to your phone with phone alerts on. Settings › Agents › Alerts sets the minutes or turns it off.
  - **Alerts** in Settings › Agents › Alerts, all optional: a notice when one tool call runs longer than a few minutes (with tool activity on), the idle nudge (below), a daily budget with notices at 80% and 100% of the amount you set, and notices when your Claude plan's five-hour or weekly usage passes 80% and 95% (on by default; needs Wisp's status line, or yours kept through it, since the limits come from Claude Code's status line), and the same for Codex's five-hour and weekly windows, read from its session file as a turn runs. They only inform; Wisp stops an agent only when you press Stop on the spend cap card. Below them Settings shows the estimated spend today and for the last 7 days.
  - **Keep awake** (off by default): Settings › Agents › Power stops the Mac sleeping while an agent works. The display can still sleep, a session waiting on you doesn't count, and neither does one Wisp hasn't heard from in 30 minutes (an agent that died without saying so).
  - **Away and focus controls:** a row under the Agents header (in the notch and on the Agents window page) has Keep awake (now, for an hour, or while agents work), Focus 25 and Focus 50, which start a Pomodoro focus session of that length with a 5 or 10 minute break after it (click again to stop), and Mute for 1 hour, which quiets every agent alert until it ends or you click it again.
  - **Quiet hours** (off by default): Settings › Agents › Quiet hours sets a daily window that may cross midnight (10 PM to 7 AM to start) and which groups it covers: Needs you, Done and Limits. In it those alerts make no sound, nothing goes to your phone, and Done cards don't show; requests still show their card, silently. Wisp also skips a session's sound and Done card while its terminal is the app in front, and for iTerm2 and Terminal, outside tmux, only while the session's own tab is selected. That tab check asks the terminal which tab is selected, and only once you've already let Wisp control it under Automation (the jump to a tab asks for that); it never asks on its own.
  - **While you were away** (on): after the Mac wakes, unlocks or comes back from 15 minutes or more of no input, one card sums up what agents did meanwhile ("3 finished, 1 failed, 1 needs you, $2.10"). It counts runs from the session history (so finished and failed need "Keep session history" on), sessions waiting on you and requests that timed out, plus the estimated spend. Click it to open the history. Settings › Agents › Quiet hours › "Show what happened while I was away" turns it off.
  - **Status line:** Claude Code gets a status line under its prompt with the project, branch, context used, model, cost and lines changed. If you already have one, choose Keep mine (Wisp reads the figures through it) or replace it; choosing None puts yours back exactly. Settings › Agents › Advanced › "Status line under Claude Code's prompt".
  - Each session shows as a status bar with its project, branch, model, state, context, cost, tokens and lines changed, and keeps them after it finishes. Click a bar to bring its terminal or editor forward.
  - **At a glance:** each session in the Agents widget reads as a title (project and branch), "You:" with your last prompt on one line, what it's doing now ("Editing middleware.ts", "Running npm test", "Waiting for approval"), its agent and terminal tags and how long it's been in that state ("27m"). A finished session reads "Done, click to jump": in the list, clicking it brings its terminal forward; at the Wide size a click selects it as before. Above the list, your 5 h and 7 d usage windows show the percent used and the time to reset (only the windows the agent reported). The prompt text stays in Wisp's memory only (never logged, saved or sent) and is hidden whenever private mode hides details; agents that don't send it show no prompt line.
  - Approval cards for edits (Claude Code's Edit and MultiEdit, Codex patches) show the first changed lines in red and green with the change's "+N −M" counts; a Write shows the file's first lines numbered and how many lines its new content has. Preview opens the whole thing. In private mode the card shows only the changed file's name and the counts, and no Preview. Allow and Deny show their shortcut (Caps Lock + Y and N, or ⌃⌥⌘Y and N without Caps Lock), and a question's options are numbered for Caps Lock + 1–9.
  - At its Wide size (the Code profile's only widget) the Agents widget lists sessions on the left and the selected one's details on the right: model with effort and thinking, context used of the window, cost, tokens, lines, last turn and session length, branch and pull request, cache hits, usage limits, folder, Claude Code version and session ID, then its timeline and a box to draft the next prompt and paste it into that terminal (it never presses Return for you). Transcript shows the session's transcript file in Finder. The header has today's spend with a 7-day trend.
  - **Session history:** the clock button in the Wide Agents widget's header swaps the details for finished sessions, newest first, with search. Each shows the project, branch, agent, model, length, cost, lines changed and prompts; a click adds its tool counts, tests (passed, failed or stopped, result unknown when it was waiting on a permission prompt, or just ran when the agent's hooks can't tell), files changed and commands, with secrets masked. Tools, files, commands and tests come from tool hooks, so they need "Show what agents are doing" in Settings › Agents › Advanced (off by default; Copilot CLI sends finished tool calls anyway); without it a session keeps its figures and prompt count. History is kept on this Mac for 30 days, at most 200 sessions, in a file only your user can read, and never holds prompts, messages, file contents or command output; commands and file names aren't saved while private mode hides details, and they're hidden in the list then too. Turning history off stops saving; what's saved stays until you clear it. With on-device AI on, each finished turn's record (never the transcript) is summarised on your Mac: the first sentence follows Done in the notch and, with "Add the command or file name" on under Phone alerts, goes with the Done push. Settings › Agents › History turns history and summaries off and has Clear history.
  - **Command line:** Settings › Agents › Advanced › Command-line tool links `wisp` into `~/.local/bin` (and says if that folder isn't on your PATH). `wisp status` lists live sessions with branch, state, model, context, cost and the running tool, then your usage limits with when they reset and, at the current pace, when they'll run out; `--json` gives the same for scripts. `wisp watch` redraws it every second, and `wisp spend` shows today and the last 7 days by project. They read from Wisp, so it must be running, and private mode applies to them as it does to the notch.
  - **Phone and Apple Watch** (opt-in, off by default): Settings › Agents › Phone alerts sends permission requests, Needs you, Done, usage limit, budget and cloud task alerts to the free [ntfy](https://ntfy.sh) app, through ntfy.sh or your own ntfy server. Permission requests get Allow and Deny buttons that answer the agent from your phone. Alerts go when you're away (the screen is locked or the Mac has been idle for a couple of minutes) or always, each kind has its own switch, and repeats are held back. They say only the agent, the kind of request and the project ("Claude Code wants to run a command in wisp"), and not even the project while private mode hides details; "Add the command or file name" (under Advanced) adds it with secrets masked. While push is on, permission prompts wait longer for an answer (10 minutes by default), which updates the agents' hooks. Settings shows the topic with a QR code and Send test; Advanced › "Your own ntfy server" has the server and fields for an access token and a separate reply token, both kept in the Keychain. The buttons carry only the reply token, so the access token is never stored in a notification.
  - **Cloud agents** (opt-in, off by default): Settings › Agents › Cloud agents adds a Cloud lane to the Agents widget with your Codex cloud tasks (`codex cloud list`), GitHub Copilot coding agent tasks (`gh agent-task list`) and Cursor Cloud Agents (its API, with a key you paste in, kept in the Keychain). Each row shows the task, its repository, whether it's working, ready or failed, and a tick or cross for its pull request's checks (`gh pr checks`). Click a row to open it on the web; its menu opens the pull request, or applies a finished Codex task to a checkout you pick (`codex cloud apply`). A task that finishes or fails, or whose checks go red, gets a notice, and goes to your phone too when phone alerts are on and Cloud tasks is on under Settings › Agents › Phone alerts (the phone gets only the agent and the repository, never the task's title). Wisp looks every 2 minutes while a task runs, every 15 minutes for two hours after, then waits until you open the Agents widget or the Mac wakes. A source is skipped when its command isn't installed or there's no key, and `codex` and `gh` use their own logins. Claude Code's cloud sessions aren't listed: they have no list command, and Wisp couldn't confirm a branch naming rule to find their pull requests by.
  - **Private mode:** while you're on a call (a call app or a browser has the microphone, or a calendar meeting is on and the microphone is in use), commands, file paths, session names, your last prompt, edit diffs and prompt drafts are hidden in the notch, and permission cards say "Details hidden" until you press Show. Choose Never, During calls or Always in Settings › Agents › Privacy, or press the eye in the Agents widget to hide them any time. macOS doesn't tell apps when the screen is shared, so sharing without a call needs the eye or Always.
- **Automation**
  - **Your own live activities:** `wisp activity start --id build --title "Building" --symbol hammer.fill --progress 0.4` shows a pill beside the notch from any script; `update` changes its title, symbol or progress (an update for one that isn't showing fails unless it has a title) and `end` removes it. The most recently touched one shows, an activity stays at most 15 minutes after its last start or update (so a script that dies doesn't pin it), and symbols come from a fixed list (`wisp activity --help`).
  - **Links:** `wisp://open?page=tray` (home, tray, media or widgets), `wisp://profile?name=Work`, `wisp://timer?minutes=25` and `wisp://activity/start?id=build&title=Building` (also `update` and `end`), from a browser, `open` in Terminal or another app. Wisp Dev uses `wisp-dev://`. A link can never approve or deny an agent's request, since any web page can open one.
  - **Shortcuts:** Open Wisp page, Switch Wisp profile, Start Wisp timer, Show Wisp activity, End Wisp activity, and Approve or Deny agent request. Approve and Deny answer only the card on screen, and only a tool permission; a plan or question there gets "Answer this one in the notch". They ask first by default, naming the agent, what it wants to do and the tool, plus the project unless private mode hides it. Any web page can start a saved Shortcut (`shortcuts://run-shortcut`), so turning "Confirm first" off lets a link to that Shortcut answer requests without asking.
- **Dictation** (off by default): hold Caps Lock + Space (or Caps Lock + D or M, chosen in Settings › General › Dictation), speak, and let go. The text goes into the prompt draft when a session is open in the Agents view, into a question card's answer (ready to check and send), or else is pasted into the app in front (copied instead, with a notice, if you switched apps while speaking; your clipboard is put back after the paste). A quick press of Caps Lock + Space still opens the shelf, and so does a hold too short to dictate. A red "Listening" pill shows while you hold the key; Esc cancels. Speech is recognised on your Mac only: if your language can't be recognised on device, Wisp says so instead of using Apple's servers. Needs Caps Lock as the shelf key, and asks for Speech Recognition and the microphone when you turn it on.
- **Profiles that follow you**: saved Home layouts, start page and surface. Wisp starts with Everyday, Work, Code (just your agents), Stats (System Stats, Timer and Day Progress), Music and Meeting. Switch with Caps Lock + 1–9 or the notch menu. Rules switch the start page and surface automatically by front app, music or a waiting agent; a Focus rule can swap Home too. Set them up, and the look each one applies, in Settings › Shelf › Profiles.
- **Notch shell**
  - One non-activating panel per display. The black shell grows out of the notch into the shelf, like a box resizing, and shrinks back into it when it closes.
  - Opens on hover or click; Caps Lock + Space toggles it. Once open it closes when the pointer leaves, when you click elsewhere, or only with Esc and the shortcut; you choose in Settings › Notch. Right-click the notch for a short menu: Open (any page), Tools (Clipboard History, Transform Files, Mini Player, Watch a Window Region, Customise Home), Profile and Form, then Settings, Check for Updates and Quit.
  - Surfaces: Black, Glass (Liquid Glass) or Tinted.
  - **Themes:** Wisp violet (default), Midnight, Aurora (violet into teal), Ember, Mono or your own two colours. The glow can instead match the desktop picture or the playing album, or drift warmer at sunset and cooler at night.
  - Trackpad swipes on the notch: two fingers down opens the shelf, sideways on the music pill skips tracks.
  - On displays without a notch: a drawn notch, a floating island or nothing. Each display can also have its own choice (Settings › Notch › Advanced › Displays).
  - **Position:** at the top (the notch), a top corner, the bottom or the left or right edge, for every display or one at a time. Corners sit below the menu bar and the bottom sits above the Dock; on the sides Wisp becomes a notch on its side, and live activities stand upright with the value under the icon.
  - **Move the island:** drag a corner, bottom or top island (or the pill it's showing) anywhere on its display. It stays a pill, settles against a nearby edge, keeps clear of Apple's Dynamic Island and opens toward the middle of the screen. Each display remembers its spot. "Move back" in the right-click menu's Form submenu or in Settings › Notch › Advanced › Displays puts it back; a plain click still opens the shelf.
  - **Floating orb:** pull the closed notch down to tear it off into a round orb (other positions float from the right-click menu), then drag it anywhere. It settles against a nearby edge, keeps its spot per display, and the shelf, pills and cards open from it toward the middle of the screen. It shows a violet ring while an agent works and a count of waiting requests. Drop it at the top centre, or use Dock to Edge in the right-click menu, to put it back.
  - **Forms:** switch between notch, corner, bottom, side, orb and window from the button at the end of the shelf's page pill (in the window, its toolbar), the ring's Form action, Hyper+F or the right-click menu. A new form springs in; the orb's shelf, cards and pills bloom out of it and fold back into it.
  - **Window mode:** use the shelf like a normal app. Form › Open as Window in the right-click menu, or pulling the open shelf away by its edge (the strip above the pages or the margins beside them, never the pages themselves), opens its pages in an ordinary window you can move, resize and minimise. The window follows Light and Dark mode and has a sidebar: Home, Tray (with its file count) and Media, then each widget you've switched on in your Widgets order (Agents shows a count of pending approvals), then live activities such as a region watch or downloads while they run. Every page fills the window: Home is a grid of widget cards, each opening its own row, the tray a grid of larger file cards, and Media large artwork with lyrics beside it. Every widget gets its own window page, with its actions in the toolbar: Quick Toggles as large state tiles (Keep awake for an hour, two hours or until a time you pick) and action buttons; Clipboard as a searchable list with Pinned and History, a type filter and a preview column (Return copies, ⌘⌫ removes, Space previews in a narrow window); Day Progress as a timeline of your working hours with today's events on it and a working-hours popover; World Clocks as up to eight city cards with analog faces and a slider to compare another time (the notch shows the first three); Pomodoro as a large ring with 25/5 and 50/10 presets and today's finished focus sessions (only their times are kept, and a new day starts empty); Timer as the same ring with preset lengths and what's counting down; Calendar as the next seven days with Join for calls; Now Playing as the Media page; Battery as cards for the Mac and each mouse, keyboard or trackpad; System Stats as CPU and memory cards with larger charts, average and peak; Weather as the current conditions and the next hours; Notes as a full-height editor; Shortcuts as a searchable grid with favourites first; Mirror as a large camera preview; and Script as the command's whole output in a scroll view with a Run button. ⌘1 to ⌘3 pick Home, Tray and Media, ⌘0 hides the sidebar, and the toolbar has the form switcher and, on Home, Customise Home. A pill click, Open as Window or tearing the shelf off lands on the matching row, and the window reopens on the row you left. Minimising or closing it puts Wisp back into the notch, island or orb it was, with the same size and spot remembered for next time. ## Build

```sh
make build   # generate the Xcode project and build
make test    # unit tests (WispCore)
make lint    # swift-format --strict
make run     # build and launch Wisp Dev (quits any running Wisp first)
make release # universal Release DMG and Sparkle zip in build/release
make hooks   # install the lefthook git hooks
```

Debug builds are a separate app, **Wisp Dev** (`com.ayomideodewale.wisp.dev`). It has its own settings, permissions, clipboard history and agent socket, starts with first-run setup, and never checks for updates, so it never touches the installed Wisp. Each one needs its own permission grants. `make run` quits the installed Wisp too, because two copies would draw two notches and both claim Caps Lock; reopen it when you're done. If you connect agents from Wisp Dev, its hooks replace the installed Wisp's until you connect them again there.

`scripts/release.sh` builds a universal Release and writes a DMG, the zip Sparkle installs, checksums, a Homebrew cask and (with a Sparkle key) `appcast.xml`. It signs with "Wisp Dev" without the hardened runtime, or with a Developer ID plus hardened runtime and notarisation when `DEVELOPER_ID` and `NOTARY_PROFILE` are set. See the comments at the top of the script.

GitHub Actions:
- `ci.yml` lints and tests every push to `main` and every pull request (the `test` check).
- `pr-title.yml` checks that a pull request's title is a conventional commit (the `pr-title` check).
- `release.yml` runs release-please on every push to `main` and builds and publishes a release when its release PR is merged. See [Releasing](#releasing).

The app icon comes from `design/logo/wisp-icon.svg`; `design/logo/export.sh` renders it into the asset catalog.

Signing:
- Without a "Wisp Dev" code-signing certificate, builds are ad-hoc signed.
- That means macOS asks for Accessibility again after every rebuild.
- Create a self-signed "Wisp Dev" certificate in Keychain Access to keep the grant.

## Contributing

`main` is protected: every change goes through a pull request, and the `test` and `pr-title` checks must pass. Pull requests are squash merged.

1. Install the git hooks once:
   ```sh
   brew install lefthook
   make hooks
   ```
2. Work on a branch: `feat/short-description` or `fix/short-description`.
3. Commit with conventional messages (`feat: ...`, `fix: ...`, `docs: ...`).
4. Push the branch and open a pull request. The template asks for a summary, how you tested and a one-line release note.
5. Give the pull request a conventional title. Squash merging keeps only the title on `main`, and that title decides the next version: `fix:` and `feat:` bump the patch, and `!` (as in `feat!:`) marks a breaking change, which bumps the minor version.

The hooks (`lefthook.yml`):

| Hook | What it does |
|---|---|
| pre-commit | Formats the staged Swift files with swift-format, adds the fixes to the commit, then lints them with `--strict` |
| commit-msg | Rejects messages that aren't conventional commits (`scripts/check-commit-msg.sh`). Merge and revert messages pass |
| pre-push | Refuses any push to `main` (`scripts/block-main-push.sh`), then runs `make test` |

`git commit --no-verify` and `git push --no-verify` skip the hooks, but CI runs the same lint and tests, and GitHub refuses direct pushes to `main` anyway.

## Releasing

Releases come from `main` through [release-please](https://github.com/googleapis/release-please).

1. **Release PR.** Every merge to `main` updates an open "chore(main): release x.y.z" pull request. It bumps `MARKETING_VERSION` in `project.yml` and adds the merged PR titles to `CHANGELOG.md`.
2. **Version.** Below 1.0, `feat` and `fix` bump the patch (0.1.0 → 0.1.1) and `!` bumps the minor version (0.1.3 → 0.2.0). `docs`, `chore`, `ci` and `test` alone don't make a release. To force a version once, add `release-as` to the package in `release-please-config.json` and remove it after that release ships, or every release keeps that version.
3. **Merge to release.** When you're ready to ship, check the release PR (there's a checklist in `.github/PULL_REQUEST_TEMPLATE/release.md`) and merge it. release-please tags `vX.Y.Z` and creates a **draft** GitHub release with the notes.
4. **Update window notes.** Sparkle's window lists the **Release note** line of each `feat`, `fix` and `perf` pull request in the release, grouped into New, Improved and Fixed (`scripts/release-notes.sh`). A pull request without one shows its title instead; "None" leaves it out. If the notes can't be built, the window shows the GitHub release text.
5. **Build and publish.** The `build` job in `release.yml` checks out the tag and runs `scripts/release.sh`, which signs the app and the Sparkle zip and writes `appcast.xml`. It uploads the DMG, zip, checksums, appcast and cask, and only then publishes the release, marking it Latest when it is the newest version. Installed copies find the update through `releases/latest/download/appcast.xml`, which never points at a release without its files.
6. **Re-running.** If the build fails, fix the cause and run Actions › Release › Run workflow with the tag (for example `v0.2.0`). It rebuilds and replaces the files.

The build number (`CFBundleVersion`) is `major × 10000 + minor × 100 + patch`, so 0.2.0 is 200 and 1.0.3 is 10003. Sparkle compares this number, so minor and patch stay below 100.

### Pulling a bad release

1. Stop new installs: in GitHub Releases, mark the previous good release as Latest (`gh release edit v0.2.0 --latest`), or delete the bad release. The appcast URL then points back at the good one.
2. Ship a fix as a new, higher version. Sparkle never downgrades, so people who already installed the bad build stay on it until the fix arrives.
3. Merge the fix and its release PR as usual; the new release becomes Latest again.

### Maintainer setup (once)

These secrets live in the repository settings (Settings › Secrets and variables › Actions). Run the commands yourself so keys never pass through anyone else.

1. **Signing certificate.** Export "Wisp Dev" from Keychain Access (right-click › Export, `.p12`, with a password), then:
   ```sh
   base64 -i WispDev.p12 | gh secret set WISP_DEV_P12
   gh secret set WISP_DEV_P12_PASSWORD
   ```
   Every release must use this same certificate, or installed copies lose their permissions.
2. **Sparkle key.** The EdDSA key is in your login Keychain under the account `wisp`. Its public half is `SUPublicEDKey` in `project.yml`. Get Sparkle's tools from its [releases](https://github.com/sparkle-project/Sparkle/releases) (same version as in `project.yml`), then:
   ```sh
   ./bin/generate_keys --account wisp -x sparkle-key.txt
   gh secret set SPARKLE_PRIVATE_KEY < sparkle-key.txt
   ```
   Keep a backup of `sparkle-key.txt` somewhere safe, such as a password manager, then delete the file. **If this key is lost, installed copies can never verify another update.**
3. **release-please token.** Create a [fine-grained personal access token](https://github.com/settings/personal-access-tokens/new) for this repository only, with Contents, Pull requests and Issues set to Read and write. Then `gh secret set RELEASE_PLEASE_TOKEN`. release-please opens its PR as you, so CI runs on it; the default `GITHUB_TOKEN` would not trigger CI. Leave "Allow GitHub Actions to create and approve pull requests" off. If you give the token an expiry, set a reminder before it lapses: an expired token makes release PRs stop updating without any error you'd notice. Revoke it if it ever leaks.
4. **Notarisation (optional, later).** With a paid Apple Developer account, add `DEVELOPER_ID_P12`, `DEVELOPER_ID_P12_PASSWORD`, `DEVELOPER_ID`, `APPLE_ID`, `APPLE_TEAM_ID` and `APPLE_APP_PASSWORD`. The workflow then signs with the Developer ID, turns on the hardened runtime and notarises, and the first install no longer needs "Open Anyway".

**Switching to a Developer ID later:** Sparkle accepts an update signed with a new certificate as long as the EdDSA key stays the same. Change the certificate or the Sparkle key in a release, never both at once. People grant Accessibility once more after that update, because the signing identity changes.

Agent hooks, the OpenCode plugin and the Claude Code status bar talk to Wisp over a Unix socket on your Mac; nothing about your sessions leaves it. The OpenCode plugin passes on session ids, the folder, tool names and the command, file or address a request is about, never prompts, replies, diffs or file contents, and answers permission requests through OpenCode's own local server. Tool activity labels are made and masked on your Mac too. The approval history is a second file there, readable only by your user: each request's tool, the card's masked one-line summary (none while private mode hides details), its session and project, how it ended and when, kept 7 days and at most 200, and removed by Clear history. While a Codex turn runs, Wisp watches that session's file for changes and reads only its token and limit figures, as it already did when a turn ended. Agent sounds you choose are played from where they are; Wisp keeps only their paths. To skip a sound or Done card you would already see, Wisp checks which app is in front and, for iTerm2 and Terminal (only once you have allowed Wisp to control them under Automation), asks it for the selected tab's terminal device name, such as ttys003; it is compared and dropped, never kept or sent. The "While you were away" card is built on your Mac from the session history, the approval history and the spend figures, and how long you were idle comes from the same input idle time phone alerts read. Compact now and the spend cap's Stop only paste `/compact` or press Esc in the session's terminal on your Mac. Private mode checks which apps hold the microphone on your Mac; it sends nothing anywhere. "Copy diagnostics" only puts Wisp's version, the macOS version, the Dynamic Island setting, your Mac's model identifier, whether each display is built in, and its screen measurements on the clipboard; nothing is sent. The `wisp` command reads the same socket, and `wisp activity` posts to it. `wisp://` links and Shortcuts actions run on your Mac and send nothing anywhere. Always allow… only writes Claude Code's settings files on your Mac. Dictation records only while you hold its key and turns speech into text on your Mac (on-device recognition is required, never Apple's servers); the audio never leaves the Mac, and neither the audio nor the text is kept. Pasting into the app in front goes through the clipboard, marked so clipboard managers, Wisp's history included, skip it, and your previous clipboard is put back a moment later. Watch a window region uses Screen Recording to grab one frame of the window you picked every few seconds; the frame is cropped, compared (and read with on-device text recognition for a text rule) and dropped straight away. Nothing is saved to disk or sent anywhere. Capture text grabs one frame of the area you drag over, leaving Wisp's own windows out, reads it with on-device text and barcode recognition and drops it; clipboard images are read the same way. The text only goes to the clipboard and is never logged. Summarise PDF reads up to 30 pages of the PDF's text on your Mac and gives it to the on-device AI below. Download progress reads only the names and sizes of files in the folders it watches (plus the size numbers in a Safari download's Info.plist), never their contents, and sends nothing; macOS may ask once before Wisp can see the Downloads folder. Per-app volume reads the sound of only the apps you turn down and plays it straight back at your level; it's never saved or sent, and the only thing Wisp checks is its loudness, to tell whether it can hear the app at all. Wisp also reads which processes are playing sound to list the apps. Up Next reads the playing playlist's name and the next songs' titles, artists and lengths from Music while the window's Media page shows it; nothing is sent.

Cloud agents only reads: it lists tasks the agents already run in the cloud and sends nothing about your local sessions. The `codex` and `gh` commands talk to their own services; Wisp never sees their tokens. Wisp doesn't log task titles, command output or the Cursor key.

Phone push is the one feature that sends anything about your agents off the Mac, and only when you turn it on. ntfy topics on a public server have no password: anyone who knows the topic can read the alerts and post to it. Wisp makes each topic from 128 random bits, uses a separate topic for replies, and accepts a reply only if it carries the single-use code of a request that is still waiting; anything else posted there is ignored, and nothing in a reply is used beyond that code and Allow or Deny. For alerts only you can read, use your own ntfy server or an access token (Settings › Agents › Advanced › "Your own ntfy server").

On-device AI never goes online. Apple Intelligence runs Apple's model on your Mac through the Foundation Models framework. Ollama is only reached on this Mac: Wisp accepts only a `localhost`, `127.0.0.1` or `::1` address, checks it before every request, uses no proxy and refuses redirects. Prompts and answers aren't logged or saved. AI text actions read the selection only when you pick an AI action in the ring, and fall back to the clipboard only when it isn't marked concealed or transient; the text goes to the chosen on-device engine and nowhere else.

Quick add reads your request on your Mac, or with the on-device AI above. Reminders and events go into your own Reminders and Calendar through EventKit (which syncs them however those apps are set up), only after you confirm the card. Wisp doesn't log what you type or what it adds.

Lyrics and artwork are on by default because the Now Playing feature looks broken without them. They follow the "feature lookups" rule in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md): visible feature only, minimum data, public endpoint, cached, own switch, listed here. Everything else that goes online stays off until you turn it on.
